Front Matter

The Position

p. 003

Every collective of one-night events leaves behind an archive it does not own. This is our attempt at the opposite: a record that is ours, that is scarce, and that admits when the experiment failed.

Negative results have almost nowhere to go. A journal of failures has no prestige to trade, so nobody builds one, so the failures are repeated at scale, forever, by people who never learned they were failures. That is publication bias, and it is expensive.

A journal of failures produced as a fashion magazine — on heavy stock, with real peer review, in a numbered and capped run — has a different kind of prestige. It is the only kind we could stomach charging for. The science stays open online. The object stays rare. We think both facts are load-bearing.

We will not launch this issue in print until an editor who is not the collective agrees to sign it. A journal edited by the collective it documents is a zine. A zine is a fine thing. This is not that. The masthead therefore reads Editor: to be named, and it will stay that way until it is true.

Standing Section 01 · Peer Reviewed

Negative Results

Two to four papers per issue
pp. 010–041

Real submissions, really peer reviewed, really published. The results that were true, uninteresting to a career, and therefore never written down anywhere else. Each paper below carries the reviewers' names and its own rejection history.

NR·01

No detectable effect of a widely marketed nootropic stack on working memory across 214 healthy adults: a pre-registered null

A. Okonkwo, L. Serrano, R. Feld · Community Lab Cohort · Received under C7 · Two rounds of review

Abstract

We tested the eight-ingredient formulation most frequently discussed in self-experimentation forums against placebo in a pre-registered, double-blind crossover (n = 214, twelve-week washout). Across n-back accuracy, reaction time, and a delayed-recall composite, we observed no effect distinguishable from zero at any dose (all 95% confidence intervals spanning the null; largest standardized effect d = 0.04). Subjective alertness rose in both arms identically. We report the full negative in the interest of the several thousand people currently buying this compound monthly. The pre-registration predicted a moderate effect. It was wrong, and we are the only people who will tell you so.

NR·02

Fourteen attempts to reproduce a headline gut-microbiome–mood correlation, and why none of them worked

M. Bright, S. Oduya · Depositor-linked cohort · Received under C7 · One round of review, one dissent

Abstract

A frequently cited 2019 finding reported a strong association between a single bacterial genus and self-reported low mood. Using three independent sampling protocols and two sequencing pipelines across fourteen re-analyses, we recovered the association exactly zero times; the original signal appears to have been a batch effect confounded with collection season. We publish the fourteen failures in full, including the two where we were briefly convinced we had found something and had not. The dissenting reviewer argues our sampling window was too narrow to close the question. We agree it is not closed. We argue it is a great deal more open than the literature currently admits.

The Polygenik Institutional Review Board's determinations for the period, reprinted in full with dissents. The board has no power and issues its opinions anyway. Its verdicts carry no legal weight whatsoever.

Case 001 · Submitter: Polygenik (self-referral) Sitting: First Quarter

Determination on "Communion" — a shared-culture drop

Approved with Conditions

The panel finds the proposed work outside the Common Rule and within its own advisory jurisdiction. It is approved subject to four conditions: written informed consent from every participant in plain language; a named on-site clinician for the duration; exclusion of anyone under 18; and destruction of all biological material within 72 hours. The conditions were imposed and, per the collective's own reporting in Case Files this issue, followed. The panel notes that a collective publishing an adverse condition against its own drop is the only reason to trust the next verdict.

Dissent · one seat

I would have withheld approval pending a written destruction receipt rather than a stated intention. Intentions are not evidence. The receipt now exists; my objection stands as a matter of sequence.

Case 007 · Submitter: withheld Sitting: Second Quarter

Determination on a proposed self-administered peptide protocol

Not Approved

The submitter proposed an unsupervised self-experiment involving repeat self-injection of a compound with no human safety data and a plausible immunogenicity risk. The panel declines to approve. It notes explicitly that this determination is an opinion, carries no legal force, and cannot compel the submitter to stop. It observes only that the submitter's own reversibility answers on the form were blank, and that the act of leaving them blank is the finding.

Standing Section 03 · Documented properly

Case Files

The drops, on the record
pp. 056–071

The events, documented as protocols rather than press: what was planned, what happened, and the specific place where reality diverged from the run-of-show. The gap between the two columns is the article.

Communion — Drop 002

One night · 88 participants · IRB Case 001, Approved with Conditions

Protocol

Participants contributed a cheek swab on arrival; samples were pooled, cultured on-site behind glass, and the resulting shared plate was photographed hourly and destroyed at 72 hours per the IRB conditions.

What went wrong

Contamination. A single unmasked contributor introduced a mold that overtook four plates by hour nine. The "shared culture" everyone watched grow was, for the back half of the night, mostly one person's kitchen.

What we learned

The failure was more legible than the success would have been. Guests understood contamination the moment they saw it eat the plate. We kept the ruined plates in the photographs. We did not reshoot.

The Celebrity Meat Tasting — Drop 003

One night · 40 seats · Withdrawn menu item, documented

Protocol

A tasting built around cultured cell lines with consented, non-identifying provenance. Twelve courses, each plated with a printed provenance card and a consent reference number.

What went wrong

Course seven never left the kitchen. The provenance chain for one line could not be verified to the standard we had published, so we pulled it mid-service and told the room why, out loud, between courses.

What we learned

Announcing the pulled course was better theatre than serving it. The rigour was the piece. This issue reprints the empty provenance card as the course-seven plate.

Standing Section 06 · The fashion editorial

Retracted

Twelve pages, shot on textiles
pp. 090–101

Camouflage cut from the evidence

Twelve pages of fashion editorial, shot on textiles printed with figures pulled directly from retracted papers. The recurring motif is the duplicated Western blot — the same band spliced across lanes it was never run in, the single most common fingerprint of image manipulation — rendered at fabric scale as camouflage. Worn, it does exactly what it did in the paper: it hides in plain sight until you know what you are looking at.

The garments are unwearable in the useful sense. They are meant to be photographed, printed on heavy stock, and kept. The styling notes on the reverse cite the case number and the date of retraction for every figure used.

Figures sourced with the work of the retraction watchdogs. Credited by name and case in the printed edition.

Standing Section 07 · In memoriam

Obituaries

Failed biotech, written properly
pp. 104–109

Failed biotechnology companies, written as proper obituaries: cause of death, survived by, in lieu of flowers. We do not use real company names. The deaths, however, are composited from real ones.

Halcyon Therapeutics, Inc.

Founded Y-9 · Wound down Y-1 · Aged 8

Halcyon Therapeutics died peacefully in its Series C, surrounded by a syndicate that had stopped returning its calls. Once valued at nine figures on a single surrogate endpoint, it is survived by a confirmatory trial that read out negative eleven weeks after the term sheet lapsed.

Cause of death
Accelerated approval, then withdrawal. The drug worked on the biomarker and not on the patients.
Survived by
Four hundred participants, a landlord, and a mechanism of action that remains, technically, elegant.
In lieu of flowers
The family asks that you read the confirmatory data before you write the press release.

Meridian Genomics

Founded Y-6 · Dissolved Y-1 · Aged 5

Meridian Genomics passed after a long illness widely misdiagnosed as product-market fit. It leaves behind a platform that could sequence anything and a market that wanted only one thing, cheaper. The pivot deck, its ninth, is being held in lieu of a headstone.

Cause of death
A platform in search of a product, outrun by an incumbent who simply lowered the price.
Survived by
Its intellectual property, now grazing quietly in a patent troll's portfolio.
In lieu of flowers
Please do not build another platform. Build the one product first.
