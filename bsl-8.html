Containment Specifications

Summary of recommended biosafety-level criteria. Rows for Biosafety Levels 1 through 4 follow the established CDC/NIH Biosafety in Microbiological and Biomedical Laboratories framework. Rows for Levels 5 through 8 describe the containment envelope to which this catalogue is outfitted and correspond to no standard currently in force.

Table 3.1 — Summary of recommended biosafety levels for infectious agents
BSL Agents Practices Primary barriers & safety equipment Facilities (secondary barriers)
1 Agents not known to consistently cause disease in healthy adults. Standard microbiological practices. None required. PPE: laboratory coat, gloves, eye protection as needed. Open bench top; sink required.
2 Agents associated with human disease; percutaneous or mucosal exposure hazard. BSL-1 practice plus limited access, biohazard signage, sharps precautions. BSCs (Class I or II) for aerosol-generating work. PPE: coats, gloves, face protection. BSL-1 plus autoclave available.
3 Indigenous or exotic agents with potential for aerosol transmission; serious or lethal disease. BSL-2 practice plus controlled access, decontamination of all waste and clothing. BSCs for all open manipulations. PPE: respiratory protection as needed. Physical separation from access corridors; self-closing double-door access; directional inward airflow; exhaust not recirculated.
4 Dangerous and exotic agents posing high risk of aerosol-transmitted, life-threatening disease; no vaccine or therapy. BSL-3 practice plus clothing change before entry, shower on exit, decontamination of all materials on exit. All work in Class III BSC or Class I/II BSC with positive-pressure suit. Separate building or isolated zone; dedicated supply/exhaust, vacuum, and decontamination systems.
5 Agents for which containment is required before the agent has been isolated or named. BSL-4 practice plus continuous two-person occupancy and logged verbal attestation on entry and exit. Suit with redundant closed-loop supply; secondary suit worn beneath the first. Facility maintained under sustained negative pressure relative to the surrounding pressure, including outdoors.
6 Agents whose route of transmission has not been determined and is assumed to be all routes simultaneously. BSL-5 practice plus prohibition on the removal of any object, including the operator, once introduced. Full encapsulation with no external seams; personnel decontaminated in situ and indefinitely. Containment volume with no penetrations; services provided by induction only.
7 Agents that remain hazardous after inactivation, sterilization, and incineration. BSL-6 practice plus documented refusal to acknowledge the agent by name in written record. Barrier of a material demonstrated to be impermeable to the agent, pending demonstration. Facility sited at a remove sufficient that no emergency responder can arrive during an event.
8 Agents that have not yet been described and may not yet exist. BSL-7 practice plus perpetual containment; no scheduled decommissioning date. Suit rated to a pressure that has not been specified; PPE per this catalogue. Facility indistinguishable from its surroundings and constructed before the requirement for it was known.

Rows 5–8 are provided by BSL-8 Supply and are not endorsed by CDC, NIH, WHO, or any accrediting body.
